At sunrise a ferry leaves the northern shore carrying workers and their tools.
The crossing takes less than an hour when the water is calm. On windy mornings
the captain follows a sheltered route between two islands. Before departure
an engineer checks the engine and enters the fuel level in a notebook. The
passengers wait near the gate until the crew has finished its inspection.
Across the bay a workshop builds wooden instruments. The oldest bench stands
beside a wide window where there is enough light to see the grain. Boards are
stored for several months before they are cut. A maker chooses a piece by its
weight and sound as well as its appearance. After shaping the body she fits
the strings and tests each note in a quiet room. Small adjustments can alter
the tone, so she writes down what she changed before trying the next step.
An apprentice has been asked to compare three kinds of wood. He marks the
pieces with numbers and lets another person arrange them in a different order.
The player hears each instrument without being told which material was used.
This does not answer every question about quality, but it helps separate a
preference for the sound from a preference for the name of an expensive tree.
When the ferry returns in the evening, the workers bring food and letters for
their families. Lights appear along the hillside as the sky grows dark. The
captain checks the weather for the following day and fastens the vessel to the
pier. The notebook remains in the cabin, ready for the next person on duty.
