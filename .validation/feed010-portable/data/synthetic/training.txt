The garden behind the library changes slowly through the year. In spring the
trees put out green leaves and the rain gathers in shallow pools beside the
stone path. A gardener records the dates when each plant begins to flower.
These records are useful because memory often confuses a warm afternoon with
an unusually warm season. Careful measurements show what actually happened.
In summer the building is open to visitors who come to read and to study.
There are books about history, mathematics, music, language, and the natural
world. Some readers compare old maps with modern surveys. Others examine the
notes of travellers who crossed the country on foot. Each account contains a
different view of the same landscape, and none should be accepted without
considering the conditions under which it was written.
The keeper makes a list of every item brought into the collection. A label
states where it came from and whether the original is available elsewhere.
When a damaged page is repaired, a photograph of its earlier condition is
preserved. Missing letters are marked as uncertain. A reader can therefore
distinguish the marks on the paper from the judgement of a later editor.
During the winter a small group meets to discuss the records. They prefer
clear questions which can be answered by an observation or a calculation.
A failed attempt is still worth recording when its method and limits are
understood. The group does not change a measurement simply because another
number would make a more attractive story. Patient comparison is the basis
of their work, and a new member can repeat it using the same materials.
